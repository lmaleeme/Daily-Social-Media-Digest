WhatsApp adds bill payment in India

WhatsApp has introduced a new bill payments feature for users in India, letting them find, track, and pay household and utility bills directly within the app. The tool runs on the Bharat Connect (BBPS) network and connects users to 22,722 billers spanning 30 categories, including electricity, gas, water, FASTag, insurance, credit cards, and loan repayments, while also offering bill summaries and account overviews for easier money management. This builds on existing WhatsApp functions in India such as mobile recharges, transit ticket booking, and government service chatbots, reinforcing Meta's long-running push to turn WhatsApp into an all-purpose "super app" similar to China's WeChat, which is used for shopping, transport, and payments. Progress has been slow due to regulatory hurdles and limited uptake of expanded services in Western markets, but Meta accelerated its India strategy in June by acquiring fintech company Cred and naming its founder, Kunal Shah, as WhatsApp's new head. With 800 million users in India, deeper payment integration could open new revenue streams beyond advertising and strengthen the case for Meta's 2014 WhatsApp acquisition.

YouTube updates branded content sourcing and disclosures

YouTube announced several updates to its shopping and branded content tools. First, it's piloting an experiment that automatically swaps a creator's tagged affiliate product for the same item sourced from a different eligible local retailer, aiming to test whether faster local delivery boosts sales and commissions; this only applies to Shopping Affiliate program tags, not videos with Paid Product Placement enabled. The test follows YouTube's recent expansion of that affiliate program to include Amazon listings. Separately, YouTube is overhauling branded content disclosure to help creators meet varying international regulations: new, clearer branded content labels will show viewers context instantly, while creators gain settings to restrict sponsored content by age or location, letting them accept brand deals previously blocked by regional legal constraints. YouTube is also introducing automated detection that can apply disclosure labels to newly uploaded videos if its systems spot undisclosed branded content, enforcing compliance without manual creator action. Additionally, the platform updated its paid product placement policy with new terminology aligned to broader industry branded-content standards, giving creators and brands clearer, more consistent rules for sponsorship arrangements.

TikTok adds voice and image carousel comments

TikTok has introduced three new comment features aimed at boosting engagement on video posts. Voice comments let users aged 18 and older record audio responses up to 60 seconds long, which appear in the comment section alongside an automatically translated text version; this feature is rolling out globally over the coming month. Comment polls are now available to all creators, letting them add up to five response options directly within the comment section, with results trackable in real time as votes come in—though only the original video creator can create a poll on their post. Photo-based comments are also expanding: users can now attach up to nine images to a single comment, which display as a tappable, scrollable carousel, and live photos can be shared as active-image replies. Standard photo comments are already live worldwide, while the carousel format is being phased in over the next month. Collectively, these tools give viewers more expressive, low-effort ways to react to content beyond typed text.

Reddit expands AI-powered Max campaigns

Reddit has made its AI-powered Max campaigns available to all advertisers, expanding beyond the select group that tested the automated ad tool since its January launch. Max campaigns automate targeting, creative selection, ad placement, and budget allocation using Reddit's audience insights, which analyze user interests and engagement to improve accuracy. Reddit is also adding three new features: API support so advertisers can build and manage Max campaigns through third-party tools, a new integration with Smartly that lets its users create Max campaigns directly, and "asset groups," which let advertisers organize multiple creative sets—each with its own headlines, media, and destination URLs—within a single campaign, useful for brands managing multiple product lines like running shoes versus training apparel. Reddit reported that during beta testing, advertisers using Max campaigns achieved 17% lower cost-per-acquisition and more than 27% additional conversions on average compared to standard campaigns. The expansion is aimed particularly at brands unfamiliar with Reddit's advertising ecosystem who may have previously hesitated to invest due to a lack of platform expertise.

X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X announced that, starting immediately, U.S. creators participating in its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs must receive payouts through X Money, with no alternative payment method offered domestically. X says the switch lets creators access funds instantly rather than waiting for a payout threshold, but it also requires phone number verification and an active X Premium subscription to open an X Money account—though Premium was already required for monetization eligibility, so little changes there. Non-U.S. creators will still be paid via Stripe. X Money exited beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to resolve regulatory issues, with Cross River now powering accounts and a linked Visa debit card for ATM withdrawals. X had struggled to gain money-transmitter approval in several states, partly due to its ties to Middle Eastern investors, making the Cross River deal a key breakthrough, though X Money remains U.S.-only for now. The push comes as TechCrunch reported a rise in hacking attempts targeting X accounts specifically to steal X Money payment information, adding security pressure as X pursues its "everything app" ambitions.

TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL have renewed their partnership with a multi-year agreement designed to deepen the league's presence on the app and give fans new ways to interact with football content. The deal introduces NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership tool, which allows leagues to create dedicated in-app spaces where fans can browse trending videos, complete fan tasks for exclusive perks, and engage around live game moments. TikTok will also expand its library of game highlights, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from both the league and individual teams throughout the year. On the advertising side, brands will get new opportunities to align with NFL-related trends through TikTok's Pulse Premiere program. The announcement comes as TikTok reports a 32% year-over-year increase in posts using the #NFL hashtag, reflecting football's growing footprint on the platform. TikTok has also previously noted that 59% of its users find watching sports content in-app more entertaining than watching live games, signaling a broader shift in how fans consume and engage with sports.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube has rolled out a new Connected TV update that lets users control their TV viewing experience directly from their phones instead of relying solely on a remote. The update syncs phone and TV apps automatically, removing the previous requirement to share a Wi-Fi network or manually pair devices. Viewers can now type comments, like videos, read descriptions, and subscribe to channels from their phones while content keeps playing on the TV screen. They can also browse other parts of the YouTube app simultaneously, including searching for videos or watching Shorts, and can send videos straight to their TV from their phone's homepage feed by tapping the three-dot menu and selecting "Play on different device." This builds on YouTube's recent string of CTV upgrades, including podcast viewing, account locking, and channel-based "stations," as well as expanding AI-powered search to smart TVs. The move responds to YouTube's growing CTV usage, with over a billion hours of content watched daily on TV sets, making remote-free interaction a practical priority for the platform.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest published a report Tuesday positioning itself as a healthier alternative to other social platforms for teen users, emphasizing that young users engage purposefully rather than scrolling endlessly. The company reported that 77% of active teen users utilize its search bar to explore interests, with teens accessing settings and feed controls at nearly double the rate of other age groups. Teens also over-index in art-related searches by 1.26 times compared to other demographics, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, and bedroom décor. Pinterest announced it will expand its distraction-free "school hours" nudge feature, launched in April 2025, to more than 30 countries after reaching 4.5 million teens across the US, Canada, France, UK, Germany, and Brazil. The company is also growing its (Me)dia Mindfulness digital literacy hub and launching a new trauma-informed resource called "Protect Your Energy," alongside increased philanthropic funding for youth mental health initiatives and school-based mental health resource connections. The timing appears to position Pinterest favorably against Meta, currently facing scrutiny over teen safety failures, despite the awkward tension of promoting reduced app usage while depending on advertising revenue.
