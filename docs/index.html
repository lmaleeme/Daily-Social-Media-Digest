X provides holiday campaign tips

X held a webinar last week sharing holiday advertising guidance for marketers, emphasizing that attention and shopping intent peak simultaneously on the platform while users remain unusually receptive to brand messaging during this period. The company presented data on last year's holiday shopping engagement trends, peak activity windows, and audience demographics to help advertisers time campaigns effectively. X also addressed the growing role of AI in shopping decisions, advising brands to describe their products in plain, comparable language so AI models can easily summarize and recommend them. For ad creative, X recommended keeping copy to 50-100 characters (roughly one sentence), writing in a conversational tone rather than overselling, and using urgency tactics like limited-time offers paired with clear calls to action. The platform also advised eliminating distractions that pull focus from the primary conversion goal, directing traffic to a single destination like a website or app. Notably, X said advertisers should avoid tagging other accounts, since tags offer an "exit" from the ad, and confirmed that hashtags and multiple emojis remain prohibited in paid ads for similar reasons.

Meta says advertisers should let campaigns gather data

Meta released a holiday season best-practices guide advising advertisers to stop tweaking ad campaigns prematurely and instead give its ad system sufficient time and data to optimize performance. The guide stresses treating each campaign setup like a detailed brief, with clearly defined optimization goals, budgets, bid strategies, and attribution settings from the outset. Meta's key data point: ad sets generating fewer than roughly 50 conversions per week ran about 28% more expensive per purchase, since the algorithm lacks enough signal to target efficiently below that volume. The company also cited last year's shopping trends, noting 42% of consumers said they'd planned to cut holiday spending, yet overall spend rose 4.2% year over year. Meta recommends using A/B testing or Conversion Lift studies to properly evaluate results rather than making reactive changes based on early dashboard fluctuations. The overall message asks marketers to trust Meta's automated systems more and intervene less, arguing that clear upfront instructions and patience—rather than constant monitoring—drive stronger peak-season campaign outcomes.

Meta delays business terms update over data scraping concerns

Meta has delayed its updated commercial terms of service, originally set to take effect Oct 30, pushing the launch to Nov 20 after backlash from publishers, advertisers, and regulators. The dispute centers on language permitting Meta to use bots, crawlers, and automated tools to access and periodically pull data from any domain or webpage connected to a business's use of Facebook or Instagram, then use that content to develop, train, and maintain its products. Critics, including the European Publishers Council and the UK's Competition and Markets Authority, warned this could effectively authorize Meta to scrape virtually any business website for AI training purposes. Meta called the backlash a misunderstanding, insisting the terms merely consolidate existing language for clarity and aren't tied to AI training; last week it already revised the wording to remove explicit training references. The company faces existing lawsuits over alleged use of copyrighted material to train its AI models, which intensified scrutiny of this update. Meta says businesses can still block crawlers via robots.txt files and that existing legal protections remain intact, but it now must explain the terms further to EU and UK authorities before relaunching.

Meta offers creative ad targeting for specific audiences

Meta has rolled out a new Marketing API feature allowing third-party platforms and marketers to attach audience targeting details, specifically age range and gender, to individual creative elements like images and videos, rather than applying those parameters only at the ad set level. This means a financial services brand could designate retirement-planning videos for users aged 50 to 65 while keeping savings account content open to all ages, or a fashion retailer could point women's sweater images toward female users and men's sweater images toward male users. By embedding targeting instructions directly into the creative, Meta's ad delivery system can more precisely match specific visuals to specific users, potentially boosting relevance without marketers needing to build separate campaigns for each demographic slice. The change took effect this week. One possible complication is gift-buying behavior, especially heading into the holiday shopping season, since restricting creative by gender or age could limit visibility to people purchasing items for someone outside that target group, raising questions about how this might affect overall campaign reach and conversions during peak shopping periods.

YouTube adds temporary pause for hacked accounts

YouTube has introduced an automated temporary pause feature designed to protect creators from account hijacking. When the platform detects unusual or unauthorized activity on a channel, it will immediately restrict several functions: uploading new content, starting livestreams, adding videos to playlists, creating custom thumbnails, and switching videos between private, unlisted, and public settings. This is meant to stop hackers from quickly locking out real owners and posting unwanted videos or streams. During the pause, YouTube's security team reviews the account, typically within 24 hours; if ownership is verified, full access returns immediately, and if hacking is confirmed, YouTube contacts the creator with recovery steps. Importantly, viewers can still watch and engage with existing channel content during the restriction, and monetization remains unaffected. Affected creators will see a notification banner inside YouTube Studio explaining the restriction, with a link to more details about the suspicious activity and guidance on recovering the account. The update adds a layer of security for creators who have invested years building audiences and revenue streams on the platform.

Australia prepares to sue social platforms over age violations

Australia is moving toward legal action against major social platforms for failing to enforce its under-16 social media ban, which took effect in December 2025 as the world's first law of its kind. Communications Minister Anika Wells, who also serves as the nation's online safety regulator, confirmed she is collecting evidence for federal court proceedings against Meta, TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube. While platforms reported removing or suspending hundreds of thousands of suspected underage accounts after the law passed, early data suggested most young users still found ways to access these apps. The regulations require platforms to take "reasonable steps" to block underage users, but the vague wording leaves room for companies to contest liability, and the government has not specified which enforcement methods count as sufficient. To strengthen pressure on noncompliant platforms, Australia recently doubled the maximum penalty for violations to $70 million per breach. With legal action now looming, platforms may face stronger incentive to adopt more rigorous and verifiable age-verification systems rather than risk costly court battles and reputational damage.
