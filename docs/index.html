What marketers should know about the legal risks of algorithmic pricing

Legal expert Laura Brett of Simpson Thacher & Bartlett outlines mounting legal risks for marketers using algorithmic pricing, which sets different prices for different customers based on data like browsing history, location, or loyalty status. New York Governor Kathy Hochul must decide by Dec. 31 whether to sign the One Fair Price Act, a broad ban on individualized pricing. This follows a December 2025 controversy where Consumer Reports found Instacart charging some shoppers up to 23% more for identical items, prompting New York Attorney General Letitia James to demand pricing records even after Instacart halted the practice. Brett distinguishes "dynamic pricing" (same price shown to everyone, varying by time or demand) from riskier "surveillance" or "personalized pricing" (different prices per customer). Over 20 states have pending bills, the FTC has issued a draft policy statement on the issue, and a House Oversight probe has sought records from Uber and Lyft. New Jersey allows private lawsuits with treble damages. Brett advises marketers to audit data inputs, map pricing practices across jurisdictions, and involve legal counsel early, since transparent, criteria-based loyalty discounts carry lower risk than device- or location-based pricing or shared third-party pricing tools that could suggest competitor coordination.
