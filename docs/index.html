X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X announced that, starting immediately, U.S. creators earning through its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs must receive payouts via X Money, with no alternative payment option offered domestically. X says this allows instant access to funds upon payment rather than waiting for a payout threshold, but it also requires creators to verify their phone number and maintain an active X Premium subscription—though Premium was already mandatory for monetization eligibility. Non-U.S. creators will keep using Stripe for now. X Money launched in beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to resolve regulatory issues; Cross River now powers X Money accounts, including a Visa debit card enabling ATM withdrawals, meaning X Money relies on Cross River's approved infrastructure rather than operating independently. X previously struggled to secure money transmitter licenses in several states, partly due to concerns over its Middle East-linked stakeholders. The push toward mandatory X Money usage comes as TechCrunch reported this week that hackers are increasingly targeting X accounts specifically to steal X Money payment information, adding pressure as X pursues its broader "everything app" ambitions built around integrated payments.

TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok has struck a new multi-year partnership with the NFL that deepens the league's presence on the platform and introduces fresh ways for fans to engage. The deal brings NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership tool, which helps leagues establish an in-app presence where fans can discover content, interact with trending videos, complete fan tasks for exclusive rewards, and gather around key moments. Beyond the hubs, TikTok will host more game highlights, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from both the league and individual teams throughout the year. Advertisers also gain new access to NFL-related trends through TikTok's Pulse Premiere offering. The expansion follows data TikTok shared showing #NFL-tagged posts rose more than 32% over the past year, reflecting football's growing traction among its users. TikTok has also cited research finding 59% of its users consider watching sports content on the app more entertaining than watching live games, a signal the company points to as evidence of shifting fan behavior that leagues should factor into their broader engagement strategies.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube has rolled out a new Connected TV feature that lets viewers control their TV-based YouTube experience directly from their smartphones, eliminating the need to navigate clunky remote controls for tasks like typing comments, reading descriptions, or subscribing to channels. The update syncs phone and TV app activity in real time, so actions like liking a video, leaving a comment, or subscribing to a channel can happen instantly without interrupting playback. Viewers can also keep using their phone's YouTube app independently while watching TV, browsing Shorts, searching for new videos, or sending clips straight to the TV screen via a "Play on different device" option in the three-dot menu. Notably, phones and TVs no longer need to share the same Wi-Fi network or be manually linked. The update arrives as YouTube says users now watch over a billion hours of content on TVs daily, prompting a wave of recent CTV upgrades including podcast support, account locking, themed video "stations," and expanding AI-powered voice search to more smart TVs beyond those with microphone-equipped remotes.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest released a report Tuesday detailing how teens use the platform and reinforcing its positioning as a mindful alternative to other social apps. The company said 77% of active teen users proactively use the search bar to explore interests, with teens over-indexing in art searches by 1.26 times compared to other age groups, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, sketch tutorials, and bedroom décor. Teens also access settings and feed controls at nearly twice the rate of other users. Pinterest is expanding its distraction-free school-hours nudge feature, launched in April 2025, to more than 30 countries after it reached 4.5 million teens in the U.S., Canada, France, the U.K., Germany, and Brazil. The company is also broadening its (Me)dia Mindfulness digital literacy hub and launching a new trauma-informed resource called "Protect Your Energy," alongside increased philanthropic funding for youth mental health initiatives and school-based mental health resource connections in the U.S. The report appears aimed partly at contrasting Pinterest's approach with Meta, which faces ongoing scrutiny over teen safety practices.
