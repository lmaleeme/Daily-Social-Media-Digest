Why brands struggle with influencer measurement — and what to do about it

The Association of National Advertisers' latest report on influencer marketing, "Reducing Waste and Optimizing Investment," found that 66% of surveyed marketers identified measurement as their biggest influencer marketing challenge, well above issues like vetting or contracting creators, which each drew 40-45%. ANA's Leah Marshall attributes this to inconsistent metric definitions across social platforms and no standard way to account for audience overlap across channels, making comparisons unreliable. While ANA's Aquila measurement initiative hasn't yet tackled creator marketing, many brands are compensating by paying to amplify influencer content, since paid media yields richer data; eMarketer projects paid amplification spend will hit $14.2 billion by 2027, overtaking creator-sponsored content revenue by 2028. The report recommends a 30/70 paid-to-native ratio for awareness campaigns and 70/30 for conversion, though Marshall stresses this should flex based on goals. Compounding the issue, marketers increasingly use influencers for direct commerce (via TikTok Shop, for example) and favor micro- and nano-creators (66% and 61% planned investment, per Kantar) over bigger names, yet lack centralized ownership of creator strategy internally, sometimes causing business units to compete for the same talent. The creator economy hit $37 billion in spend last year, per IAB, and is projected to reach $44 billion in 2026.

Meta offers updated ad placement control guidance

Meta has been stripping away manual ad placement controls across its platforms, meaning advertisers can no longer exclude specific placements—like Facebook search results or in-stream Reels ads—for campaigns using engagement, leads, app promotion, or sales objectives. Awareness and traffic objective campaigns still retain placement control for now, but Meta signals this will likely extend company-wide eventually, prioritizing its AI-driven ad serving system over manual advertiser input. To ease the transition, Meta published guidance outlining four workarounds: customizing creative assets (images, videos, text, aspect ratios, URLs) per placement through Ads Manager's new multi-media ad workflow; using "placement value rules" to raise or lower bids for specific placements based on business value; maintaining account-level placement exclusions by objective through existing account controls; and partnering with creators or other businesses, which still allows control over media, messaging, and placement scope at the ad creation stage. The changes come amid pushback from advertisers worried about losing campaign precision, but Meta's broader trajectory points toward full automation of placement decisions, betting that AI-optimized distribution will outperform manually controlled targeting for most advertising objectives going forward.
