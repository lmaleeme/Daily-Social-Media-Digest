X adds new anti-lawsuit provision to terms of service

X rolled out revised Terms of Service this week that every user must accept to keep using the platform. The changes route legal disputes to Texas courts under Texas law, reflecting X's integration with Elon Musk's Texas-based SpaceX. New language also holds users accountable for actions taken by autonomous features on their behalf, echoing Musk's past statements that individuals generating illegal content via xAI tools bear personal liability. Most notably, X strengthened its class-action waiver clause, now explicitly requiring users to give up rights to a jury trial and to joining any class, collective, or representative legal action, extending this to corporate affiliates where legally allowed. This update arrives as X faces a class action from sexual abuse survivors alleging xAI's Grok chatbot generated child sexual abuse material, with one plaintiff claiming Grok was trained using existing CSAM tied to them. Similar waivers exist at Meta, TikTok, and X's predecessor Twitter, and such provisions haven't historically prevented class actions from proceeding, so enforceability remains uncertain despite the timing raising questions.

Meta keeps trying to make digital assistants happen

Meta released Muse this week, its most advanced AI assistant app yet, allowing users to name their bot, interact with it, and delegate tasks it can complete autonomously in the background. CEO Mark Zuckerberg frames Muse as a step toward "personal superintelligence," describing his own use case as wanting the assistant to help him be a better father, husband, and friend. However, this isn't Meta's first attempt at this concept: in 2015 the company launched "M," a similar AI assistant that Messenger chief David Marcus said could book restaurants, arrange travel, and purchase gifts, but user interest never materialized and Meta shelved it in January 2018. Given this track record, Muse's odds of widespread adoption appear slim, which could undermine Meta's massive AI investment strategy. The recurring failure suggests a mismatch between Zuckerberg's optimization-driven mindset and most users' preference for organic human interaction over automated task delegation.

Meta launches Muse personal AI

Meta has released Muse, an AI-powered personal agent app and in-stream chatbot that goes beyond conversational suggestions to actively complete tasks such as trip planning, ticket booking, form-filling and in-stream purchases. Users can name their agent and link it to their Facebook and Instagram profiles for added context, with messaging available through the standalone app or directly in WhatsApp. To address privacy concerns around Meta collecting more personal data, Muse operates on a separate "Muse Secure VM" that isolates user data and the agent, a system built with input from Signal co-founder and former WhatsApp encryption architect Moxie Marlinspike. Meta is also building an "ideas library" that recommends tasks based on how similar users engage with Muse, since CEO Mark Zuckerberg noted many people don't know how to use AI agents effectively. Zuckerberg added that Muse can connect to Meta ad accounts to offer marketing guidance across Meta's platforms, and that agents will eventually learn from each other across the user base. Muse is free, with 100 million tokens available per week for testing.

Pinterest renames campaign objectives to simplify ad focus

Pinterest is overhauling the naming of campaign objectives within its Ads Manager platform to help advertisers more easily match campaign types to business goals. The previous "Conversions" and "Catalog Sales" objectives are being renamed to "Leads," "Sales," and "Consideration" depending on context, with Pinterest clarifying that Leads campaigns target non-transactional actions like signups, while Sales campaigns focus on transactional actions such as add-to-carts and checkouts. Pinterest also released an updated objective guide to help advertisers choose the right campaign type for their goals. The company confirmed this is purely a naming and clarity update, not a functional change, meaning advertisers won't see any shifts in campaign performance or ramp times, which will still depend on creative sources as before. Existing campaigns built under the old Conversions and Catalog Sales labels will remain editable until April 30, 2027, after which they'll be archived. While a minor structural tweak, the change is meant to reduce confusion for both current advertisers and newcomers setting up Pin campaigns on the platform.

Will removing algorithmic sorting improve social media?

Australia's government is pushing forward a policy that would force social media platforms to make algorithmic feed sorting an opt-in feature rather than the default, giving users the choice to view chronological content instead. The move responds to growing evidence that algorithmic amplification promotes divisive, rage-inducing content and worsens political polarization. However, platforms including Meta have long argued that engagement drops sharply without algorithms—Facebook noted back in 2014 that with algorithmic ranking, users read 70% of available stories versus just 57% without it, since the average user faced roughly 1,500 potential feed stories per visit. Meta's repeated internal testing since then has consistently shown chronological feeds reduce reading, likes, and comments compared to algorithmic sorting. The article argues that while removing algorithms might hurt platform engagement metrics, it could benefit users by reducing time spent scrolling and exposure to divisive content, though it may also increase content overwhelm and echo chambers. With user behavior toward curating feeds having evolved significantly since 2014, Australia's smaller market could serve as a useful real-world testing ground for measuring the actual impact of removing algorithmic defaults.

Australia moves to enable algorithm opt-outs

Australia's government announced Tuesday it will pursue legislation requiring social media platforms to notify both new and existing users, giving them an explicit choice between an algorithm-driven default feed or a chronological one showing only content from accounts they follow. Currently most platforms bury opt-out settings in menus, and only X and Threads offer a "Following" feed as a selectable default, and only on desktop. This policy would make Australia the largest real-world test of algorithm-free social media, potentially revealing how much engagement-based recommendation systems contribute to misinformation spread and divisive content amplification. Beyond feed defaults, the government also plans new rules targeting addictive app features and AI chatbots, plus stricter controls on content involving eating disorders, crime, gender equality issues, and dangerous stunts. Regulators would be authorized to fine noncompliant platforms up to $109.2 million, with detailed rules to be finalized in coming months. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese's office framed the move as boosting protection from harmful content while expanding user choice. The change could influence global policy debates and force publishers to rethink audience-engagement strategies if algorithmic feeds lose their default dominance.

YouTube expands Watch With to more creators

YouTube is widening access to Watch With, a feature first tested in 2024 alongside its NFL livestream coverage, that lets creators record live reactions to other broadcasts. Creators in the expanded experiment group now see a "React live" button in the Share panel on eligible mobile livestreams; tapping it locks their phone camera into horizontal orientation (with no option to disable the camera) and begins broadcasting their reaction. Viewers watching from any device see a combined vertical stream showing both the original content and the reactor's camera feed simultaneously. Creators using the tool can still monetize their reaction streams normally and gain access to shared viewing metrics from the original broadcast. This rollout is part of YouTube's broader push into interactive live content, alongside other tests like gamified livestream leaderboards, 24/7 "livestream stations," and expanded live gifting options. The push comes as YouTube reported in September 2025 that over 30% of daily logged-in users watch live content, underscoring live viewing as a growing engagement and monetization priority for the platform.

Pinterest offers advice on creating effective shopping promotions

Pinterest released a four-step framework to help brands run more effective shopping promotions ahead of the year-end retail season, tied to its growth to 640 million monthly active users, most of whom use the app for product discovery. WhatsApp adds bill payment in India

WhatsApp has introduced a new bill payments feature for users in India, letting them find, track, and pay household and utility bills directly within the app. The tool runs on the Bharat Connect (BBPS) network and connects users to 22,722 billers spanning 30 categories, including electricity, gas, water, FASTag, insurance, credit cards, and loan repayments, while also offering bill summaries and account overviews for easier money management. This builds on existing WhatsApp functions in India such as mobile recharges, transit ticket booking, and government service chatbots, reinforcing Meta's long-running push to turn WhatsApp into an all-purpose "super app" similar to China's WeChat, which is used for shopping, transport, and payments. Progress has been slow due to regulatory hurdles and limited uptake of expanded services in Western markets, but Meta accelerated its India strategy in June by acquiring fintech company Cred and naming its founder, Kunal Shah, as WhatsApp's new head. With 800 million users in India, deeper payment integration could open new revenue streams beyond advertising and strengthen the case for Meta's 2014 WhatsApp acquisition.

Reddit expands AI-powered Max campaigns

Reddit has made its AI-powered Max campaigns available to all advertisers, expanding beyond the select group that tested the automated ad tool since its January launch. Max campaigns automate targeting, creative selection, ad placement, and budget allocation using Reddit's audience insights, which analyze user interests and engagement to improve accuracy. Reddit is also adding three new features: API support so advertisers can build and manage Max campaigns through third-party tools, a new integration with Smartly that lets its users create Max campaigns directly, and "asset groups," which let advertisers organize multiple creative sets—each with its own headlines, media, and destination URLs—within a single campaign, useful for brands managing multiple product lines like running shoes versus training apparel. Reddit reported that during beta testing, advertisers using Max campaigns achieved 17% lower cost-per-acquisition and more than 27% additional conversions on average compared to standard campaigns. The expansion is aimed particularly at brands unfamiliar with Reddit's advertising ecosystem who may have previously hesitated to invest due to a lack of platform expertise.
