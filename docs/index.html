X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X has made X Money the mandatory payout method for U.S. creators enrolled in its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs, eliminating alternative payment options domestically. X says the change lets creators access funds instantly rather than waiting for payout thresholds. To qualify, creators must verify their phone number and maintain an X Premium subscription, though Premium was already required for monetization eligibility. Non-U.S. creators will continue receiving payments via Stripe. X Money launched in beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank, which now underpins its accounts and Visa debit card enabling ATM withdrawals. TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL have expanded their partnership through a new multi-year agreement designed to deepen the league's presence on the app and boost fan interaction. The deal introduces NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership framework, letting fans discover trending content, complete fan-based tasks for exclusive perks, and engage around live game moments. TikTok will also host more highlights, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from the league and individual teams throughout the year. Advertisers gain new opportunities too, with access to NFL-related trends through TikTok's Pulse Premiere ad offering. The announcement comes as TikTok reports #NFL-tagged posts grew over 32% in the past year, and 59% of users say watching sports clips on TikTok is more entertaining than watching full games live, signaling a broader shift in how fans consume and engage with sports content.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube rolled out an update allowing viewers to control their Connected TV experience directly from their smartphones instead of relying solely on a remote or game controller. The update syncs engagement features like comments, likes, descriptions, and channel subscriptions between phone and TV screens, while still letting users browse Shorts, search videos, or scroll their homepage feed on mobile. A new 'Play on different device' option in the three-dot menu lets users send videos from their phone straight to their TV. Notably, phones and TVs no longer need to share the same Wi-Fi network to sync. This follows YouTube's push into CTV, where users now watch over a billion hours of content daily on TV sets. Recent related updates include podcast viewing, account locking, and channel-specific 'stations,' plus expanding AI-powered search to smart TVs — a feature now more accessible without needing a microphone-equipped remote or high-end TV model.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest published a report Tuesday detailing how teens use its platform, positioning itself as a mindful alternative to apps like Meta's amid ongoing scrutiny over youth mental health. The company said 77% of active teen users proactively use its search bar, and teens access settings/feed controls at nearly twice the rate of other age groups. Teens over-index on art searches by 1.26x, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, and bedroom décor.
