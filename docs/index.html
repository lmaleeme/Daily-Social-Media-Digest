Meta offers updated ad placement control guidance

Meta has been stripping away manual ad placement controls as it shifts toward fully automated, AI-driven ad serving, with the change already applied to campaigns using engagement, leads, app promotion, and sales objectives—meaning advertisers can no longer exclude placements like Facebook search results or Reels in-stream ads for these campaign types. Awareness and traffic objectives still retain placement controls for now, but Meta signals that all objectives will eventually lose this capability. To address advertiser pushback, Meta outlined four alternative methods for maintaining some control: customizing media assets by placement through its new multi-media ad creative workflow in Ads Manager, using "placement value rules" to raise or lower bids for specific placements based on business value, setting account-level placement exclusions by objective through account control tools, and managing placements when partnering with creators or brands via Advantage+ placements and partnerships, which lets advertisers select media, text, and restrict where partnership content appears. The overarching direction shows Meta prioritizing its AI-guided targeting and delivery system over granular advertiser control, a shift marketers should factor into campaign planning and budget strategy going forward.

Meta removes access verification for developers

Meta has removed the data access verification step it introduced in 2022 for developers whose apps tap into Meta business data on behalf of clients, meaning they can now go live without submitting business details for a separate review or waiting for approval. Previously, API endpoints checked whether a business was verified as a "Tech Provider" before processing requests, rejecting calls from unverified businesses as a safeguard against misuse. Meta says it can retire this layer because its Business Verification and App Review systems have matured and now rely on more sophisticated detection signals. The original 2022 policy was part of a broader effort to close data loopholes following major privacy failures, including the 2018 Cambridge Analytica scandal, where academic data access was exploited for political targeting, and a 2020 BBC report revealing 5,000 developers mistakenly got access to Facebook profile data. Removing this verification step marks a notable shift in Meta's data governance posture, coming as the company simultaneously pushes to expand business and developer adoption of its AI tools across its platforms.

LinkedIn exec offers insight into the battle against AI slop

LinkedIn is detailing how it's fighting AI-generated "slop" content, with VP of Engineering Tim Jurka explaining the system's mechanics in an Oct. 8 post. Since launching an in-stream AI slop reporting button in August, which surpassed 1 million uses within three weeks, LinkedIn has used that feedback to train detection models through a "teacher-student" setup, where larger models identify emerging slop patterns and generate training data for faster, smaller models. The platform also deploys specialized AI agents, each governed by specific policy rules covering things like promotional intent or timeliness; when an agent hits an ambiguous case, it escalates to human reviewers and updates its own policy based on their feedback. Combining these two methods, LinkedIn says its classifiers now cover all posts distributed beyond a user's immediate network with 94% precision in flagging AI slop, building on an earlier reported 40% drop in slop impressions. The update reflects growing platform-wide pressure to curb low-value, repetitive AI content as user skepticism toward social feeds continues to rise.
