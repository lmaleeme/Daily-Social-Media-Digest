X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X announced that as of today, all U.S. creator payouts from Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions must go through X Money, eliminating other payment options domestically. The switch lets creators access funds immediately after payout rather than waiting to hit a minimum threshold. To qualify, creators must verify their phone number and maintain an X Premium subscription, though Premium was already required for monetization eligibility. Non-U.S. creators will keep using Stripe for payments. X Money launched in beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to satisfy regulatory requirements, with Cross River also powering the platform's Visa debit card for ATM withdrawals. Some states had resisted approving X's money transmitter license over ties to Middle East investors. X Money remains U.S.-only for now. TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL announced a new multi-year partnership expanding the league's presence on the platform. The deal introduces NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership tool, letting fans discover trending videos, complete fan-based tasks for exclusive perks, and engage around live game moments. TikTok will also host more game highlights, archival footage, and year-round storytelling content from the NFL and individual teams. Advertisers get new ways to reach NFL audiences through TikTok's Pulse Premiere ad offering, tied to trending football content. TikTok noted that #NFL hashtag posts grew more than 32% over the past year, showing strong engagement among its user base. The company also cited earlier research finding 59% of users consider watching sports clips on TikTok more entertaining than watching full live games, suggesting the platform is reshaping fan behavior and viewership habits, a trend sports leagues are increasingly factoring into their content and engagement strategies.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest published a report positioning itself as a healthier alternative to other social platforms for teen users, emphasizing offline engagement over endless scrolling. Teens over-index in art searches by 1.26 times, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, and bedroom décor.
