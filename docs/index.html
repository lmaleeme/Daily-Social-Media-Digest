X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X has mandated that all U.S. creators earning through its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs must now receive payouts via X Money, eliminating alternative payment methods domestically. X says this allows instant access to funds upon sending, rather than requiring creators to hit a payout threshold first. Enrollment requires phone number verification and an active X Premium subscription, though Premium was already mandatory for monetization eligibility. Non-U.S. creators will keep using Stripe for now. X Money launched in beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to satisfy regulatory requirements, with Cross River also powering the platform's Visa debit card for ATM withdrawals. X has struggled to gain money-transmitter approval in some states, partly due to its ties to Middle East investors. TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL renewed their partnership with a multi-year agreement that deepens the league's presence on the app. The centerpiece is new NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership framework, giving fans a dedicated space to discover trending content, complete fan-based tasks for exclusive rewards, and engage around live game moments. TikTok will also expand its library of game highlights, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from the NFL and individual teams throughout the year, not just during games. Advertisers get new opportunities too, through TikTok's Pulse Premiere offering, which lets brands align with NFL-related trending moments. TikTok noted that #NFL hashtag posts rose more than 32% over the past year, signaling strong engagement among its user base. The company also cited earlier research showing 59% of users find watching sports content on TikTok more entertaining than watching live games, suggesting the deal reflects a broader shift in how fans consume and interact with sports content.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube rolled out an update letting viewers use their smartphones to control the app on connected TVs, addressing the difficulty of typing comments, reading descriptions, or subscribing to channels using a standard remote. The synced connection now supports engagement features like liking, commenting, and subscribing directly from a phone while content keeps playing on the TV. Users can also browse other parts of the YouTube app simultaneously, including searching for videos or watching Shorts, and can send videos to their TV via the three-dot menu and 'Play on different device' option. Notably, phones and TVs no longer need to share the same Wi-Fi network to sync. This follows YouTube's push into CTV, where users now watch over a billion hours of content daily on TV sets. Recent related updates include podcast viewing, account locking, channel-based 'stations,' and expanding AI-powered search to smart TVs beyond those with microphone-equipped remotes.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest released a report positioning itself as a positive, wellbeing-focused alternative to other social platforms, particularly amid ongoing scrutiny of Meta's handling of teen safety. The company says 77% of active teen users proactively use its search bar, and teens access settings and feed controls at nearly twice the rate of other age groups. Teens over-index on art-related searches by 1.26 times compared to other users, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, sketch tutorials and bedroom décor, which Pinterest ties to offline project inspiration. The platform is expanding its distraction-free school-hours nudges, launched in April 2025, from six countries to more than 30; the feature has already reached 4.5 million teens in the U.S., Canada, France, the U.K., Germany and Brazil.
