YouTube adds co-view counts to CTV metrics

YouTube announced it is updating how it measures Connected TV viewership by incorporating co-viewing data into its official view counts, bringing its metrics in line with long-established television industry measurement standards. Previously, YouTube's view counts have generally reflected individual sessions or devices, but traditional TV measurement has always accounted for the fact that multiple people often watch content together on a single screen, such as a household watching a show as a group. By adopting this co-viewing standard, YouTube's CTV numbers will better reflect actual audience reach rather than just device-level engagement, giving advertisers and content creators a more comparable benchmark against traditional broadcast and cable TV metrics. Importantly, YouTube confirmed this isn't a one-time adjustment limited to CTV: the company plans to apply these updated, co-viewing-inclusive view counts consistently across all its viewing formats moving forward, not just connected television screens. This shift signals YouTube's continued push to position itself as a direct competitor to traditional TV in the eyes of advertisers and media buyers evaluating reach and engagement.
