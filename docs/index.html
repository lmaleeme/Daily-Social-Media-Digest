X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X has made X Money the sole payout method for U.S. creators participating in its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs, cutting off alternative payment options domestically. The company says this allows instant access to funds upon sending, rather than requiring creators to hit a payout threshold first. To qualify, creators must verify their phone number and maintain an X Premium subscription, though that subscription was already required for monetization eligibility, so the practical barrier isn't new. Non-U.S. creators will continue receiving payments via Stripe. X Money launched in beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to resolve regulatory issues, with Cross River now powering accounts and a Visa debit card enabling ATM withdrawals. X previously struggled to gain money transmitter approval in some states, partly due to concerns over its Middle East-linked stakeholders. The push comes as X pursues its "everything app" ambitions with payments as a core feature, even as TechCrunch reported this week that hackers are increasingly targeting X accounts specifically to steal X Money payment information.

TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL have expanded their partnership through a new multi-year deal aimed at deepening the league's presence on the platform and giving fans more ways to engage. The agreement introduces NFL game day hubs built using TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership tool, which allows leagues to create dedicated in-app spaces where fans can browse trending content, complete fan tasks for exclusive perks, and connect around key moments. Beyond game days, TikTok will host expanded highlight packages, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from the league and its individual teams throughout the year. Advertisers also gain new opportunities to align with NFL-related trends via TikTok's Pulse Premiere ad product. TikTok noted that #NFL-tagged posts grew more than 32% over the past year, reflecting strong football engagement among its user base. The company has also previously reported that 59% of its users find watching sports content on TikTok more entertaining than watching live games, a trend that signals shifting fan behavior leagues are increasingly having to address.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube has introduced a new Connected TV feature that lets users control their TV viewing experience directly from their smartphones, addressing complaints that actions like typing comments, reading descriptions, or subscribing to channels are cumbersome with a standard remote. With the update, viewers can sync their phone to their TV app to access comments, likes, and subscriptions in real time without interrupting playback, and no longer need to share a Wi-Fi network or manually pair devices. While watching TV, users can simultaneously browse Shorts, search for videos on their phone, or send content to their TV screen by tapping the three-dot menu next to a video and selecting "Play on different device." This builds on YouTube's push into CTV, where viewers now watch over a billion hours of content daily on TV sets. Recent CTV updates have also included podcast viewing, account locking, and channel-specific "stations," alongside expanding AI-powered search to smart TVs, previously limited to models with microphone-equipped remotes.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest released a report Tuesday detailing teen usage patterns and expanding several wellbeing initiatives, framing itself as a healthier alternative to platforms like Meta amid ongoing scrutiny over youth safety. The company said 77% of active teen users proactively search for interests, and teens check settings and feed controls at nearly double the rate of other age groups. Teens also over-index in art-related searches by 1.26 times, gravitating toward drawing references, anime styles, sketch tutorials and bedroom décor ideas. Pinterest is expanding its school-hours device-down reminders, launched in April 2025, from six countries to more than 30; the feature has already reached 4.5 million teens across the U.S., Canada, France, the U.K., Germany and Brazil. The company is also growing its "(Me)dia Mindfulness" digital literacy hub, launching a new trauma-informed section called "Protect Your Energy," increasing donations to youth mental health causes, and helping U.S. students access school-based mental health resources. Pinterest emphasized that teens spend less time per week on its app than on other platforms, positioning lower engagement as a wellbeing win rather than a business drawback.
