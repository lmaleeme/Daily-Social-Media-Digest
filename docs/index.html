LinkedIn explains its improved content recommendation system

LinkedIn has detailed a new recommendation system called the Profile Relevance model, designed to surface more useful and relevant posts from people outside a user's existing network. The system relies on embeddings generated by large language models, which translate profile and content signals into a format the algorithm can use to match users with posts from accounts they don't currently follow. The goal is to broaden the range of voices and perspectives users see in their feed, rather than limiting recommendations to first-degree connections or previously engaged accounts. By leveraging LLM-based embeddings, LinkedIn aims to better understand the semantic relevance between a user's interests, professional background, and the content being shared by others on the platform, even when there's no direct social or follow connection. For marketers and content creators, this signals a shift toward discoverability driven more by topical and professional relevance than by existing audience size, potentially opening new reach opportunities for posts that resonate with the right professional context rather than just existing followers.
