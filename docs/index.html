X Money will be the only payout option for U.S. creators

X announced that, starting immediately, U.S. creators participating in its Original Content Rewards and Subscriptions programs must receive payouts through X Money, with no alternative payment method offered domestically. X says the switch lets creators access funds instantly rather than waiting for a payout threshold, but it also requires phone number verification and an active X Premium subscription to open an X Money account—though Premium was already required for monetization eligibility, so little changes there. Non-U.S. creators will still be paid via Stripe. X Money exited beta in November 2025 and expanded after X partnered with Cross River Bank to resolve regulatory issues, with Cross River now powering accounts and a linked Visa debit card for ATM withdrawals. X had struggled to gain money-transmitter approval in several states, partly due to its ties to Middle Eastern investors, making the Cross River deal a key breakthrough, though X Money remains U.S.-only for now. The push comes as TechCrunch reported a rise in hacking attempts targeting X accounts specifically to steal X Money payment information, adding security pressure as X pursues its "everything app" ambitions.

TikTok signs multi-year NFL deal

TikTok and the NFL have renewed their partnership with a multi-year agreement designed to deepen the league's presence on the app and give fans new ways to interact with football content. The deal introduces NFL game day hubs built on TikTok's GamePlan sports partnership tool, which allows leagues to create dedicated in-app spaces where fans can browse trending videos, complete fan tasks for exclusive perks, and engage around live game moments. TikTok will also expand its library of game highlights, archival footage, and ongoing storytelling content from both the league and individual teams throughout the year. On the advertising side, brands will get new opportunities to align with NFL-related trends through TikTok's Pulse Premiere program. The announcement comes as TikTok reports a 32% year-over-year increase in posts using the #NFL hashtag, reflecting football's growing footprint on the platform. TikTok has also previously noted that 59% of its users find watching sports content in-app more entertaining than watching live games, signaling a broader shift in how fans consume and engage with sports.

YouTube adds mobile phone controls for CTV

YouTube has rolled out a new Connected TV update that lets users control their TV viewing experience directly from their phones instead of relying solely on a remote. The update syncs phone and TV apps automatically, removing the previous requirement to share a Wi-Fi network or manually pair devices. Viewers can now type comments, like videos, read descriptions, and subscribe to channels from their phones while content keeps playing on the TV screen. They can also browse other parts of the YouTube app simultaneously, including searching for videos or watching Shorts, and can send videos straight to their TV from their phone's homepage feed by tapping the three-dot menu and selecting "Play on different device." This builds on YouTube's recent string of CTV upgrades, including podcast viewing, account locking, and channel-based "stations," as well as expanding AI-powered search to smart TVs. The move responds to YouTube's growing CTV usage, with over a billion hours of content watched daily on TV sets, making remote-free interaction a practical priority for the platform.

Pinterest promotes its role in teen wellbeing

Pinterest published a report Tuesday positioning itself as a healthier alternative to other social platforms for teen users, emphasizing that young users engage purposefully rather than scrolling endlessly. The company reported that 77% of active teen users utilize its search bar to explore interests, with teens accessing settings and feed controls at nearly double the rate of other age groups. Teens also over-index in art-related searches by 1.26 times compared to other demographics, favoring topics like drawing references, anime styles, and bedroom décor. Pinterest announced it will expand its distraction-free "school hours" nudge feature, launched in April 2025, to more than 30 countries after reaching 4.5 million teens across the US, Canada, France, UK, Germany, and Brazil. The company is also growing its (Me)dia Mindfulness digital literacy hub and launching a new trauma-informed resource called "Protect Your Energy," alongside increased philanthropic funding for youth mental health initiatives and school-based mental health resource connections. The timing appears to position Pinterest favorably against Meta, currently facing scrutiny over teen safety failures, despite the awkward tension of promoting reduced app usage while depending on advertising revenue.
